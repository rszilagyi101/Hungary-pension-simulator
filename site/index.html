        How the simulation works

        The target is the January stock of recipients published by KSH: either all recipients of pensions, benefits and annuities (table 25.1.1.2 for 1990–1999, 25.1.1.38 from 2000) or old-age pensioners only. Regressors are the resident population on 1 January, the share and number of people aged 65 and over, life expectancy at birth and the statutory retirement age.

        Models

        ARIMA / ARIMAX fits an ARIMA(p,d,q) model, optionally on logs, with standardised regressors: the 65+ population, the retirement age and a step dummy for the 2012 reform that moved early-retirement and disability pensions into other benefit categories. Intervals come from the model.

        Holt exponential smoothing projects the level and trend of the series itself, with an optional damping factor φ that flattens the trend over time.

        Demographic ratio model splits recipients into a ratio times an eligible population. With Eurostat single-year-of-age data downloaded, the eligible population is everyone at or above the retirement age in each year, so raising the retirement age removes whole birth cohorts. Without it, the denominator is the 65+ population and each extra year of retirement age removes a set share of recipients. The ratio follows a damped linear trend over a chosen window.

        Random forest, gradient boosting, ridge regression and a neural network learn the recipients-per-65+-person ratio (or the count itself) from retirement age, the 65+ share, life expectancy, the 2012 dummy, a time trend and lagged values, and forecast recursively. Tree models cannot extrapolate beyond the retirement ages they have seen, so later increases are added through the same elasticity as the ratio model.

        Population and age structure

        The age structure comes from KSH table 22.1.1.3 (single years of age by sex on 1 January). Years KSH does not publish in the bundled file are filled from Eurostat demo_pjan after a data download, and otherwise interpolated along each birth cohort and scaled to the official total population of the year.

        The population projection is a cohort-component model by single year of age and sex. Each year, every cohort survives one year using a mortality schedule whose level is calibrated to the life-expectancy path of each sex, births come from an age-specific fertility schedule scaled to the fertility-rate path, and net migrants are added with a young-adult age profile. Starting from 2016 with the observed fertility, life expectancy and migration, the engine reproduces the published 2026 population within about half a percent. Low and high variants move fertility, migration and life-expectancy gains together.

        By default the pensioner models use this projection, and the ratio model's denominator is everyone at or above the retirement age of each year, taken directly from the single-year ages. You can switch to the Eurostat projection once downloaded, or to the simple fallback path.

        Men and women

        KSH publishes recipients by sex from 2000 (table 25.1.1.38). For each sex, the app models recipients per person at or above that sex's retirement age (women's age was lower until 2009) with a damped trend, then scales both so that men plus women equal the ensemble forecast. The split is back-tested on the hold-out years. The age tree shades everyone at or above the retirement age of the year shown.

        Scenarios

        A scenario is a set of ticked parameters, each a named change to the baseline settings (for example raising the retirement age by two years or adding 20,000 net migrants a year). Parameters are grouped as positive (expected to lower the pension burden), negative (expected to raise it) or neutral (mainly timing or structure). The burden is measured as recipients per 100 people aged 15–64. After a run, each parameter's measured effect is shown next to its expected category, either within your scenario (your scenario minus the same scenario without it) or on its own against the baseline. Back-tests are run without scenario changes, so model weights stay the same across scenarios.

        Validation and ensemble

        Each model is refitted on the history without the last hold-out years and scored on them (MAE, RMSE, MAPE). The ensemble weights models by the inverse of their MAPE, equally, or by your manual weights. Ensemble intervals are weighted averages of the model bounds, a practical approximation rather than an exact combined distribution.

        Scenario inputs

        Population after the last observed year comes from Eurostat EUROPOP (proj_25np, or proj_23np) once downloaded. Before that, a fallback path follows the UN World Population Prospects 2024 medium variant trend applied to the KSH 2026 base. You can shift the elderly population, set a future retirement age path or link it to life expectancy, and add a drift to the recipient ratio. Any future values you add in the Data tab replace the scenario values for those years.

        This web version

        Everything is calculated in your browser: the models, the population projection, the scenarios and the exported files. The server only stores the official KSH and Eurostat tables it downloads, so they can be shared by every visitor. Settings, saved scenarios and the data you add are kept in this browser (local storage) and are never uploaded. The models match the desktop app; the ARIMA model uses the exact likelihood, so results can differ slightly from statsmodels when the log option is off.

        Caveats

        Retirement ages before 2022 are an approximate average of the legislated male and female schedules; women's 40-year eligibility and other special routes are not modelled separately. 1991–2000 population values are interpolated between censuses until Eurostat data is downloaded. Results are scenarios, not official forecasts.
